# Spoken narration editing

Use everyday wording in the configured target language, suited to the selected audience. Say the useful point early, connect explanation and example, and let punctuation express sensible pauses. Keep enough context for someone hearing it once; do not turn the narration into a list of screen headings.

Read the draft as speech. Shorten sentences whose clauses are hard to follow, replace unexplained jargon with a plain explanation, and remove repeated setup. Keep language that actually explains sequence, contrast or uncertainty. Avoid mechanical word bans or deleting an informative list merely to vary its length. Do not add greetings, engagement bait or a dramatic ending unless the intended style calls for them.

After editing, verify the original factual boundaries: exact quantities/units, who made a statement, when it applied, relevant conditions, comparisons, causal versus correlational language, and what remains unknown. Do not invent a first-person story to make it personable. A hypothetical example should sound hypothetical. Claims that a method is accurate or popular require source support, not a confident tone.

These are project-specific instructions for narration writing. For Chinese narration, Humanizer-zh is a useful optional editorial reference: https://github.com/op7418/Humanizer-zh . Its current instructions were reviewed on 2026-10-17. This plugin does not bundle that skill or require installing it, and does not claim to detect authorship or guarantee any AI-detector score.

For English narration, the original Humanizer is an optional editorial reference: https://github.com/blader/humanizer . Neither external skill is bundled or required. Match idioms, punctuation and spoken number forms to the target language; do not mechanically apply Chinese sentence rhythm to other languages.
